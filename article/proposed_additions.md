# Proposed Additions to Manuscript

Based on the empirical experimental setup found in the codebase, the following additions are proposed to address the specific reviewer/reader questions in the manuscript.

### 1. Raw Computational Speed Benchmarks
**Question Addressed:** How many volumes and from where were they sourced for the raw computational speed benchmarks?

**Proposed Text Addition (in Experimental Series 1):**
> "To assess raw computational speed independently of the caching benchmark, we additionally measured isolated spatial operations across multiple scales. These tests included resampling, orientation changes, and affine transformations, which represent some of the most common geometric operations in multimodal medical image preprocessing. Benchmarks were performed on single synthetic volumes of size $256 \times 256 \times 128$ (simulating medium-resolution multi-modal patches) using both CPU and GPU backends. A synthetic $128 \times 128 \times 64$ volume was also evaluated for downscaling specific validations."

### 2. Hardware Benchmarks Specification
**Question Addressed:** Clarifying which benchmarks the specified hardware (Intel Core Ultra 9 285K and NVIDIA RTX 3090) applies to.

**Proposed Text Addition (in Experimental Series 1):**
> "Both the 100-subject caching pipeline benchmark and the isolated raw computational speed benchmarks (resampling, orientation changes, fused affine transformations) were executed on identical hardware configurations: an Intel Core Ultra 9 285K processor for CPU-bound computations and an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU for hardware-accelerated processing."

### 3. Fused Affine / Tolerance Specification
**Question Addressed:** What was the tolerance during the execution of the benchmarks?

**Proposed Text Addition (in Experimental Series 3 - Differentiability Verification):**
> "...For the fused affine interpolation kernel, the gradients obtained on the CPU and GPU were compared directly, and numerical agreement was strictly verified within a tolerance of $10^{-4}$ to guarantee floating-point consistency across backends."

*Note: For the SUV numerical agreement in double precision against SimpleITK, the verified precision tolerance was $10^{-14}$.*

### 4. Multi-modal Theranostic Transformation Parameters
**Question Addressed:** What was the plane and angle used for the batched multi-modal transformation?

**Proposed Text Addition (in Experimental Series 2 - Multi-modal Processing):**
> "...Subsequently, a geometric transformation was applied to the entire batch. A spatial rotation of $45^{\circ}$ around the Z-axis (axial plane) was executed simultaneously across all four modalities using a single batched transformation call, followed by a linear translation of 10 voxels along the X-axis to verify spatial origin tracking."

### 5. Synthetic Learned Inverse Rotation Configuration
**Question Addressed:** What was the exact configuration of the synthetic data and cases used for the learned inverse rotation experiment?

**Proposed Text Addition (in Experimental Series 3 - Differentiability Verification):**
> "To further assess end-to-end differentiability in a trainable setting, a learned inverse rotation experiment was conducted using synthetic single-case batched image configurations. A synthetic 3D structural line image was generated on a strictly confined $16 \times 16 \times 16$ voxel grid to ensure strong spatial gradient signals. A total of 120 batched sample configurations (100 for training, 20 for testing) were generated by applying random three-axis sequential rotations within an angular range of $\pm 30^{\circ}$. A lightweight 3D Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) combined with a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) head was then trained using gradient descent for 20 epochs to predict the inverse Euler rotation parameters directly from the spatial grids."
